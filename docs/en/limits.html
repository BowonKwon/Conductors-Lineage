Verification#

  • This is exploratory edition 0.2.1. The independent check of relations and numbers against the sources is not finished, and the edition may contain errors.
  • "Confirmed" means that the sentence is in the source. The source itself may be wrong; biographical dictionaries disagree with one another too.

What the independent check found#

  • A sample of 115 source rows of relations was checked separately against the sources. 59 of them could be opened. Among those, the quotation differed from the source in wording or word order, or was missing, in 6 rows; years or place were more specific than the quoted sentence in 14; the kind of relation could not be settled from the sentence alone in 10; and the subject was not stated in that sentence in 15. The rows pointed out were reopened and corrected. Rows outside the sample may have the same problems.
  • Of the 376 relations whose subject is conducting, 60 take it from the context of the biography (a conducting course, a conducting master class) and not from the quoted words. The person panel marks these "read from context."
  • Of 60 tenure rows checked, the title or a year was more specific than the cited page in 7 and 2 could not be opened. Read the title column as a research note.
  • Of 25 festival rows from the official pages, 21 matched the concert page; the others could not be opened.
  • After the second check, all rows that are drawn were gone through again. 21 rows stood as confirmed although the sentence quoted was from Wikipedia: for 17 a sentence was found in another source and substituted, and 4 were set back to candidate. Of the rows that were drawn, 4 whose sentence speaks only of influence, help or copying a score, not of study or an assistant post, were removed.
  • Before version 0.2, the 263 source rows that were still candidates in the data file were checked again against sources other than Wikipedia. For 99 rows the sentence stating the relation was read in such a source, and the row is now drawn (90 confirmed, 9 where sources disagree); for 153 no such source was found, and they remain candidates; 11 were removed (the source does not speak of teaching, the teacher's name was wrong, or the same relation was entered twice). The 114 rows that were raised, newly added or corrected were then checked separately against the sources. 58 of them could be opened. Of the 56 that could not, 51 were refused by the site (31 of these are on one site that carries a biographical dictionary); for the other 5 the address was not found, the site disallows automated collection by the checker, or the row is a Wikipedia row. After this check the kind of relation was changed in 13 rows, 2 rows were set back from "sources disagree" to candidate, and 10 rows that entered the same relation twice were merged. The rows that could not be opened rest on the reading made when the row was researched; they have not been checked separately.
  • The rule that separates formal study from short study is not applied evenly in this edition. Of the 705 relations of formal study, 117 show no word for an institution or course and no year or duration in the quoted words or in the place and years columns (the source says only, for example, that someone studied composition with a person; the number comes from a mechanical search for words that name an institution or course and for years or durations, so some rows that name an institution in a word outside the list are among them). They are kept as formal study because the source gives the person as a teacher, but how long and in what course the study took place cannot be told from this material. The rows raised from candidate in version 0.2 follow a narrower rule: where the sentence names no course, period or lessons, the relation is placed under short study (teaching at home by a father is kept as formal study). The kind of the older rows was left as it was, so the two groups do not follow the same rule.

What is not drawn#

  • The 153 candidate rows (137 found only in Wikipedia or Wikidata, 16 not confirmed in another source) are not used as evidence in the chart or the tree. For 2 of them the same relation (same teacher, pupil and kind) is on the site from another source (it can be seen in the tree on the Find a person page); the relations in the other rows are not in the chart or the tree. All of them are in the data file.
  • A missing line has several possible reasons: no sentence stating the relation was found in a source other than Wikipedia, the relation is found only in Wikipedia and kept as a candidate, or, in the lineage chart, the year of birth of one of the two people is unknown, so that person is not on screen and neither is the line. 164 of the 572 people on the list have no relation drawn at all. Many are past conductors of Korean regional orchestras whose biographies are hard to find.
  • Teachers were researched one step up only. For the 486 people outside the list, their own teachers were not researched separately (only 3 such relations, found while researching someone else, are included).
  • Influence, mentorship and merely watching rehearsals are not counted. A few relations classed as short study, however, rest on a source saying that someone learned by attending rehearsals.

Reading the numbers#

  • The number of relations leans toward people with detailed biographies. A conductor whose official biography names many teachers gets many relations; one with a short biography gets few. Do not compare conductors by their number of relations.
  • Subjects were read from the wording of the sources. In 226 of the 952 relations the source does not state the subject. For some of the others the subject was read from the name of an institution or course (a "conducting class," for instance) rather than from a sentence.
  • The line between kinds of relation is not always sharp. For an invited period of study versus assistant work, or assistant for a single tour versus a standing assistant post, the wording of the source was followed.

People#

  • 433 people have relations but no confirmed year of birth. They do not appear in the lineage chart; they can be looked up on the Find a person page. 409 of them are teachers outside the list.
  • Korean spellings of foreign names follow the Korean orthographic rules for loanwords; 329 of them are provisional, not yet checked against established usage, and are marked on the Korean pages.
  • Teachers known only by surname ("Ries," "Paul") are left as the source gives them. The same person may appear twice under different names.
  • Among the people marked as Dresden roots, for Antonio Lotti only his stay in Dresden (1717–1719) could be confirmed, not a Kapellmeister title. Giovanni Alberto Ristori's tenure as Kapellmeister in 1725–1733 is at odds with a biographical dictionary. For the three people marked "predecessor orchestra," the tenures could not be confirmed from an official history or a biographical dictionary (only Wikipedia and a secondary list).

Tenure tables and the Orchestra Festival#

  • Of the 303 rows for heads in the tenure tables, 72 rest only on a Wikipedia table or on a source not opened directly, and in 70 the sources give different years. Some people entered as heads are not on the list (Lev Steinberg, Miklós Erdélyi).
  • The 537 festival records for 1989–2020 come from a list in the Korean Wikipedia. No record was found for 2021.

The English pages#

  • For 94 Korean conductors whose own Latin spelling was not found in the sources, the surname is given in its customary spelling (Kim, Lee, Park and so on) and the given name by the Revised Romanization of Korean, marked with an asterisk (*). The conductor's own spelling may differ. Where a conductor's own spelling is in the sources, it is used.
  • 11 foreign teachers collected from Korean-language sources, whose Latin spelling was not found, appear in Hangul on the English pages too. So do foreign conductors on the Orchestra Festival page who are not on this site's list.
  • English names of Korean orchestras are literal translations and may differ from the orchestras' official English names. Place and career notes are translations of notes written in Korean.

Not in this edition#

  • A recording layer, asking whether teacher and pupil take similar timings when they record the same work, is left for a later edition.
  • Please report errors and missing sources through the issues of the GitHub repository.
