What this page shows Look up a person by name and see that person's teachers and pupils as a tree, three generations each way.

What you can see 1,010 people can be found. 823 have at least one relation drawn (confirmed or disputed); 187 are on the list but have no relation drawn yet.

Take care A teacher two generations up is a teacher's teacher, not someone the person studied with. The tree also follows piano and composition teachers. Turn on "Conducting teachers only" to keep only relations of conducting study or assistant work. Relations whose source does not state the subject drop out too, so the filter does not keep every conducting teacher.

Examples, starting with the most connected

  • Formal study — studied for some time in a conservatory course or in private lessons. Relations that a source states without an institution or a period are mostly placed here as well.
  • Assistant — worked under that conductor as assistant conductor, associate conductor or répétiteur (the pianist who coaches opera rehearsals and assists the conductor). A line is drawn only when the source names the senior conductor. The arrow runs from the senior to the junior. Concertmasters are not counted.
  • Short study — a master class, a summer course or another brief period of study. Cases where a biography says only that someone was taught by a person, naming neither a course nor a period, and cases of learning by attending rehearsals are placed here too.
  • Sources disagree — the sources give different years, kinds of relation or facts, or one of them denies the relation. Both are shown.

How to read the tree: the selected person is in the middle, teachers are to the left (one generation up is a direct teacher or the conductor the person assisted) and pupils to the right. Line colors and patterns follow the legend above. A dashed box is a person outside the list, that is, someone who is not on the list and appears only in a relation (mostly as a teacher). Select a box to move to that person; the source sentence for each relation is shown below.
