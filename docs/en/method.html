What counts as a relation#

There are three kinds of relation. The arrow always runs from the one who taught (or the senior) to the one who learned (or the junior).

  • Formal study: studied for some time in a conservatory course or in private lessons. Where a source gives a person as a teacher without an institution or a period, and nothing marks the study as a short course, the relation is mostly placed here as well (120 relations; see the Limits page).
  • Assistant: worked under a conductor as assistant conductor, associate conductor or répétiteur (the pianist who coaches opera rehearsals and assists the conductor). A line is drawn only when the source names the senior conductor. If a source says only that someone was an assistant conductor without saying under whom, no line is drawn and the post is noted as career. Concertmasters are not counted.
  • Short study: a master class, a summer course, an invited period of study or the like. Where a biography says only that someone was taught by or studied with a person, without naming a course or a period, the relation is placed here too, since nothing shows a formal course. So is a source saying that someone learned by attending rehearsals.

Where a source says only that someone was a mentor, gave advice or was an influence, no relation is counted.

Subject. For each relation, what was studied with that teacher is read from the wording of the source (conducting, composition, theory, an instrument and so on). If the source does not say, the subject is "not stated." A subject was never set to conducting merely because the teacher was a famous conductor. "Conducting teachers only" keeps the relations whose subject includes conducting, plus the assistant relations.

Who is included#

The list holds 572 people: 467 chosen as conductors, and 105 of their teachers whose dates were researched separately. Another 438 people are not on the list and appear only in a relation ("outside the list"; 436 of them only as the teacher); their dates were not researched separately (only the 41 whose year of birth turned up in other research have one).

  • Heads of 17 orchestras. Principal conductors and music directors of the Staatskapelle Dresden, Gewandhausorchester Leipzig, Vienna Philharmonic, New York Philharmonic, Boston Symphony, Berliner Philharmoniker, Leningrad (St. Petersburg) Philharmonic, Royal Concertgebouw Orchestra, Chicago Symphony, Czech Philharmonic, Philadelphia Orchestra, London Symphony, Cleveland Orchestra, London Philharmonic, Moscow Philharmonic, Seoul Philharmonic and KBS Symphony.
  • Added conductors. Heads of other major orchestras and opera houses and conductors of period performance (playing with the instruments and practices of the composer's time), who would be missed by the rule above.
  • Korean conductors. Past and present heads of Korean symphony orchestras, conductors who appeared at the Orchestra Festival in 2022–2026, and conductors with international posts or competition prizes. 171 people on the list are recorded under Korean names. Conductors working full time with Korean traditional orchestras or choirs are not included.
  • Teachers one step up. The teachers of people on the list are included; their own teachers were researched only when they are on the list in their own right.

People marked separately. "Roots" are court Kapellmeister from before conducting became a profession of its own, and early-generation composers and performers who also conducted: 14 people who led music at the Dresden court before 1733 (Heinrich Schütz among them; for Antonio Lotti only his stay is confirmed, not a Kapellmeister title), 9 later early figures in Dresden, Leipzig and elsewhere, and 16 of the first Korean generation. The 3 who led the predecessors of the Leningrad Philharmonic (the Court Orchestra and State Orchestra, 1882–1920) are marked "predecessor orchestra." 5 conductors of Korean descent with foreign nationality and no post as head of a Korean orchestra are marked with the reason they were included (shown when the person is selected on the Find a person page).

What counts as confirmed#

  • Confirmed: the text of a source other than Wikipedia was opened and the sentence stating the relation was read. That sentence is shown beside the relation. It means that the sentence is in the source, not that the fact is beyond dispute.
  • Sources disagree: sources give different years or kinds of relation, or one of them denies the relation. Both are shown.
  • Candidate: found only in Wikipedia (227 rows), or seen only in a search-result excerpt or on a site that disallows automated collection, whose evidence was removed (38 rows). Candidates are not drawn in the chart or the tree; they are in the data file only.

In a relation where sources disagree, a source row that denies the relation (4 rows) is marked as denying it. Of the 880 source rows behind the 854 confirmed and disputed relations, 16 give a paraphrase because the original sentence could not be copied verbatim (marked "paraphrase" on screen), 45 are abridged or have a word supplied in parentheses (shown with an ellipsis or parentheses) and 14 have no quotation.

How the material was collected#

Sources were read one public page at a time. Sites that state that automated collection is not allowed were not opened (each site's robots.txt, its notice to automated programs, was read first). Where such a rule was discovered after collection, the evidence from that site was removed and the relation was set back to candidate. Wikipedia and Wikidata were not used as evidence to confirm a relation. Wikipedia is carried as it stands in four places. Three are on screen: the side that disagrees with another source in a "sources disagree" relation (13 rows), the Orchestra Festival list of 1989–2020, and rows of the tenure tables and their data file that rest only on a Wikipedia table (115 rows, none of them confirmed). One is in the data file only: 227 of the candidate rows. No photographs or record covers are reproduced.

Tenure tables of the heads of 17 orchestras#

These tables were the starting point of the list. Of the 303 rows for heads (principal conductors and music directors), 161 were confirmed from a sentence in the orchestra's official history or a biographical dictionary, 70 are rows where sources give different years (so they are entered as two or more rows) and 72 rest only on a Wikipedia table or on a source that was not opened directly. The 85 rows for principal guest conductors, conductors laureate and assistant conductors are in the data file only.

The title column is a research note. Most titles follow the wording of the source, but some rows add a title that the cited page does not give. Kapellmeister and Hofkapellmeister are the (court) music director of an orchestra, GMD (Generalmusikdirektor) is general music director and Chefdirigent is principal conductor. In the citations, NDB and ADB are the German biographical dictionaries Neue Deutsche Biographie and Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie, DBI is the Italian Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani, and Baker's is Baker's Biographical Dictionary of Musicians. Many citations and titles are notes written in Korean.

Data files#

The tables of this site can be downloaded as CSV (UTF-8).

  • persons.csv: 1,010 people, with ID, Korean and Latin names, years of birth and death, Wikidata ID, on or outside the list, marks.
  • relations.csv: every source row of a relation, candidates included, with teacher, pupil, kind, status, subject, years, place, source, quotation, address and date checked.
  • tenures.csv: tenure tables of the 17 orchestras.
  • festival.csv: appearance records of the Orchestra Festival. festival_news.csv: rows from newspaper articles compared with the Wikipedia list.
